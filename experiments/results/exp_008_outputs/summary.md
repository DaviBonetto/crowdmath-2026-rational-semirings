# Experiment 008 — Bounded Valuation Patterns

## Purpose

This experiment computes bounded $p$-adic valuation vectors for nonzero elements of $\mathbb{N}_0[q]$. For a nonzero rational $x = a/b$ in lowest terms, the $p$-adic valuation is $v_p(x) = v_p(a) - v_p(b)$, where $v_p(n)$ is the exponent of $p$ in the prime factorization of $n$. Valuation patterns help organize denominator behavior, divisibility structure, and arithmetic properties relevant to Exercises 0.8, 1.7, and 1.8.

## Bounded Warning

> **This is a bounded computational sample. It does not prove the complete valuation structure of the infinite monoid.**

Selected primes are chosen from primes appearing in $q$ and in the bounded sample. The prime list is capped at 8 primes. Valuation ranges reflect only elements reachable within the degree and coefficient bounds.

## Parameters

- `max_degree = 5`
- `max_coeff = 3`
- `prime_cap = 8` (first 8 primes appearing in $q$ and sample)
- Sample generated by `generate_n0_q(q, max_degree, max_coeff)` from `semirings.py`
- Valuation functions from `src/crowdmath2026/valuations.py`
- q values tested: 2, 1/2, 1/6, 3/2, 2/3, 4/9, 5/3

## Interpretation

**q = 2:** Elements lie in $\mathbb{N}_0[2] = \mathbb{N}_0$. All denominators are 1, so $p$-adic valuations are nonnegative. The valuation vector tracks divisibility by small primes within the integer sample.

**q = 1/2:** Negative 2-adic valuations appear. Elements include $1/2, 1/4, 1/8, \ldots$ whose denominators are powers of 2. This reflects the structure $\mathbb{N}_0[1/2] \subset \mathbb{Z}[1/2]_{>0}$.\n\n**q = 1/6:** Both negative 2-adic and 3-adic valuations appear. Elements include denominators that are products of powers of 2 and 3. This reflects $\mathbb{N}_0[1/6] \subset \mathbb{Z}[1/6]_{>0}$.\n\n**q = a/b with a, b > 1:** Numerator and denominator primes of $q$ create mixed valuation behavior. For example, $q = 3/2$ introduces both 3-adic and 2-adic valuations in sample elements. The valuation vector changes with each power of $q$.

**Valuation data organizes future experiments.** Divisibility patterns, denominator bounds, and prime support are useful inputs for IDF analysis and atomic divisor detection.

**This is not a proof of infinite valuation behavior.** The sample covers only elements reachable within the degree and coefficient bounds.
