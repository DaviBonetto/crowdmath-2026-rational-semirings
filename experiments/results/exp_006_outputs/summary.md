# Experiment 006 — Candidate Atomic Divisors

## Purpose

This experiment searches for **candidate atomic divisors** of selected target elements in bounded samples of $S_q = \mathbb{N}_0[q]\setminus\{0\}$ under multiplication. An element $a \in S_q$ is a **candidate multiplicative atom** if it is a nonunit with no nontrivial multiplicative factorization within the bounded sample. A **candidate atomic divisor** of a target $x$ is a candidate atom $a$ such that $x/a$ also lies in the sample. Candidate atomic divisors are grouped up to sample associate classes. This experiment prepares for Experiment 007 (IDF sanity checks).

## Bounded Warning

> **This is a bounded computational sample. It does not prove the complete atomic divisor structure of the infinite monoid.**

Bounded samples may miss atomic divisors whose quotient lies outside the generated sample. Candidate atoms are not proven atoms: a factorization may exist outside the bounded search range.

## Parameters

- `max_degree = 5`
- `max_coeff = 3`
- `max_abs_unit_exponent = 5`
- `max_targets_per_q = 8`
- Sample generated by `generate_n0_q(q, max_degree, max_coeff)` from `semirings.py`

## Interpretation

This experiment prepares for IDF sanity checks by building the candidate atomic divisor picture for each $q$ case.

**Atomic divisors must be counted up to associates.** If two candidate atoms $a$ and $b$ are associates (i.e. $a = ub$ for a sample unit $u$), they count as one divisor in factorization length sets.

**q = 1/2 and q = 1/6:** Nontrivial units merge visible divisors into fewer associate classes. The number of associate classes of atomic divisors may be strictly smaller than the raw count of candidate atoms dividing a target.

**q = a/b with a, b > 1:** Theory (Exercise 1.7) predicts only unit $1$. Divisor associate classes are singleton in the bounded sample.

**Candidate atoms are not proven atoms.** A bounded sample candidate atom may fail to be a true atom if a multiplicative factorization exists outside the sample.

**Bounded samples may miss divisors** whose quotient requires elements outside the generated sample.
