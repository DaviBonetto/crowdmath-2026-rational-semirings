# Experiment 002 — Bounded Unit Detection

## Purpose

This experiment detects units inside bounded samples of $(\mathbb{N}_0[q]\setminus\{0\}, \cdot)$ by checking whether multiplicative inverses are also present in the generated sample. An element $x$ is a **sample unit** if $1/x$ also appears in the same bounded sample. Results are compared against the theoretical unit structure from Exercise 1.7 (Resource 1).

## Bounded Warning

> **This is a bounded computational sample. It does not prove the full unit group or any infinite mathematical property.**

Bounded samples can miss units whose inverses require elements outside the generated sample (false negatives). False positives are rare but possible if the inverse appears in the sample by coincidence for a theoretically non-unit element.

## Parameters

- `max_degree = 5`
- `max_coeff = 3`
- `max_abs_unit_exponent = 5`
- Sample generated by `generate_n0_q(q, max_degree, max_coeff)` from `semirings.py`
- q values tested: 2, 1/2, 1/6, 3/2, 2/3, 4/9, 5/3

## Interpretation

**q ∈ ℕ (q = 2):** Theory predicts the only unit is $1$. The bounded sample of $\mathbb{N}_0[2] = \mathbb{N}_0$ confirms this.\n\n**q = 1/n:** The unit group of $(\mathbb{N}_0[1/n]\setminus\{0\},\cdot)$ is generated by the prime factors of $n$. Bounded samples may miss theoretical units whose inverses exceed the sample range. For $q=1/2$, units include $2$ and $1/2$; for $q=1/6$, units include $2, 3, 1/2, 1/3$, and their products within the degree bound.

**q = a/b with a, b > 1:** Theory (Exercise 1.7) states the only multiplicative unit of $(\mathbb{N}_0[q]\setminus\{0\},\cdot)$ is $1$. The bounded sample confirms this.

**Unit detection is a prerequisite for studying associates and IDF.** Two elements are associates if they differ by a unit factor. Without knowing the unit group, associate classes and length sets cannot be classified.
